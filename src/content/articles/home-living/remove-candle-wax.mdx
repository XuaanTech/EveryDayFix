# How to Remove Candle Wax from Any Surface

Candle wax spills are frustrating but very fixable. Whether it landed on your carpet, tablecloth, wood furniture, or clothing, the key is the same: harden the wax, scrape off the bulk, then use heat or solvents to remove the rest.

The exact technique depends on the surface, but every method uses items you already have at home.

## General Steps (All Surfaces)

1. **Don't panic, and don't wipe hot wax.** Let it cool and harden completely — usually 15-30 minutes. Wiping hot wax just spreads it and makes it worse.
2. **Scrape off the bulk.** Use a dull knife, credit card, or plastic scraper. Metal can scratch some surfaces, so be careful on wood and delicate fabrics.
3. **Remove the residue** using one of the surface-specific methods below.

## How to Remove Wax from Carpet

Carpet is the most common place for wax spills, and thankfully one of the easiest to treat.

1. **Let the wax harden completely.**
2. **Scrape off** as much as you can with a butter knife or spoon.
3. **Place ice cubes** in a zip-lock bag over the remaining wax to make it brittle. Wait 2-3 minutes, then scrape again.
4. **Iron method:** Place a brown paper bag (or several layers of paper towel) over the stain. Iron on **low heat** for 10-15 seconds. The wax will melt and absorb into the paper.
5. **Move the paper** to a clean section and repeat until all wax is absorbed.
6. **Vacuum** the area to restore carpet fibers.

> **Colored wax:** If the carpet is still stained after removing the wax, dab with a small amount of rubbing alcohol and blot with a clean cloth.

## How to Remove Wax from Clothing

1. **Freeze the garment** by placing it in the freezer for 30 minutes, or hold ice against the wax spot.
2. **Scrape off** the hardened wax carefully.
3. **Place the stained area between two paper towels** or a paper towel and a brown paper bag.
4. **Iron on medium heat** — the wax will melt and transfer to the paper.
5. **Repeat** with fresh paper until no more wax transfers.
6. **Wash the garment** as normal. If a dye stain remains, treat with a stain remover before washing.

> **Important:** Don't put the wax-stained garment in the dryer until you're sure all the wax is removed. Heat from the dryer can set the stain permanently.

## How to Remove Wax from Wood Furniture

Wood requires more care to avoid damaging the finish.

1. **Scrape gently** with a plastic scraper or old credit card — never use metal on wood.
2. **Place a plastic bag with ice** over the wax to harden any remaining residue. Scrape again.
3. **Dampen a cloth** with warm water and mild dish soap. Wipe the area gently.
4. **For stubborn residue,** apply a small amount of wood-safe cleaner or a dab of mayonnaise (the oils dissolve wax). Wipe clean after 5 minutes.
5. **Dry immediately** with a soft cloth.

> **Warning:** Avoid using heat (like an iron) directly on wood — it can damage the finish.

## How to Remove Wax from Glass and Tile

Glass and tile are non-porous, making wax removal straightforward.

1. **Scrape off** the wax with a razor blade held at a 45-degree angle (glass only — use plastic on tile).
2. **Spray with window cleaner** or wipe with warm, soapy water.
3. **Buff dry** with a clean cloth.

## How to Remove Wax from Fabric Upholstery

1. **Scrape off** the hardened wax.
2. **Place an ice cube** in a cloth and press over the remaining wax.
3. **Use a hair dryer on low heat** to melt remaining wax while blotting with a clean white cloth.
4. **Blot with rubbing alcohol** for any remaining stain. Test on an inconspicuous area first.

## Prevention Tips

- Place candles on a heat-resistant tray or plate.
- Use candle holders with wide bases.
- Keep candles away from drafts that cause uneven burning and dripping.
- Trim wicks to 1/4 inch before lighting to reduce dripping.

## Comparison

| Surface | Best Method | Difficulty | Risk of Damage |
|---------|-------------|------------|----------------|
| Carpet | Iron + paper bag | Easy | Low |
| Clothing | Freeze + iron | Easy | Low |
| Wood | Plastic scraper + soap | Medium | Medium |
| Glass | Razor blade + cleaner | Easy | Very low |
| Upholstery | Hair dryer + blotting | Medium | Medium |

## The Bottom Line

For most surfaces, the **ice-then-iron method** works perfectly: harden the wax, scrape off the bulk, then use heat and paper to absorb the rest. Always let wax cool before touching it, and avoid heat on wood surfaces.
