# How to Prevent Frozen Pipes in Winter

A burst pipe from freezing can cause thousands of dollars in water damage in minutes. The good news: preventing frozen pipes is cheap, simple, and takes a single afternoon of work before winter arrives.

## Why Pipes Burst

When water freezes, it expands by about 9%. Inside a sealed pipe, this expansion creates enormous pressure — enough to split copper, PVC, and even steel. The burst often doesn't happen where the ice forms, but downstream where pressure builds up.

The worst part: you often don't know a pipe has burst until the ice thaws and water starts flowing — sometimes while you're away from home.

## Prevention Steps

### 1. Insulate Exposed Pipes

Foam pipe insulation sleeves cost $1-3 per foot and are the single most effective prevention measure.

- **Where to insulate:** Pipes in the basement, crawl space, attic, garage, and along exterior walls.
- **How to install:** Slide the foam tube over the pipe and press the self-adhesive edge closed. Cut to length with a utility knife.
- **Extra protection:** In very cold climates, wrap insulated pipes with heat tape for added warmth.

### 2. Seal Air Leaks

Cold air reaching pipes causes freezing. Seal entry points:

- **Caulk or spray foam** around pipe penetrations in exterior walls.
- **Seal cracks** in the foundation, basement, and crawl space walls.
- **Weatherstrip** doors and windows near exposed pipes.

### 3. Prepare Outdoor Plumbing

- **Disconnect all garden hoses** from exterior faucets.
- **Shut off interior supply valves** for outdoor spigots (if your home has them).
- **Drain remaining water** from the spigot by opening it after shutting off the supply.
- **Install insulated faucet covers** on exposed outdoor spigots.

### 4. Keep Water Moving

Moving water freezes much more slowly than standing water.

- **During extreme cold** (below 20°F / -7°C), let faucets drip slightly — just enough to see movement.
- **Prioritize faucets** served by pipes in exterior walls or unheated areas.
- **Open both hot and cold lines** — even a trickle helps.

### 5. Use Indoor Heat

- **Open cabinet doors** under kitchen and bathroom sinks, especially those on exterior walls.
- **Keep the garage door closed** if water pipes run through it.
- **Maintain consistent heat** — set the thermostat no lower than 55°F (13°C), even at night or when away.

### 6. Know Your Main Shutoff

Find your main water shutoff valve before you need it. If a pipe does burst:

1. **Shut off the main valve** immediately.
2. **Open all faucets** to drain the remaining water.
3. **Call a plumber.**

## How to Thaw a Frozen Pipe

If you suspect a pipe is frozen but hasn't burst:

1. **Open the faucet** served by the frozen pipe.
2. **Apply heat** starting from the faucet end and working toward the frozen section.
3. **Use a hair dryer** (move it back and forth — don't hold it in one spot).
4. **Wrap pipes in hot towels** or use a heating pad.
5. **Never use an open flame** (blowtorch, propane heater) — this is a fire hazard and can damage the pipe.

## The Bottom Line

**Foam pipe insulation** is the best investment — it costs a few dollars per foot and prevents most freezing. Combine it with **sealing air leaks**, **disconnecting outdoor hoses**, and **letting faucets drip** during extreme cold, and your pipes will be well-protected all winter.
