# How to Fix a Running Toilet

That constant hissing or trickling sound from your toilet tank isn't just annoying — it's wasting up to 200 gallons of water per day. A running toilet is one of the most common and most fixable plumbing problems.

With under $10 in parts and 30 minutes, you can solve it yourself.

## How a Toilet Tank Works

Understanding the parts helps you diagnose the problem:

- **Flapper:** A rubber seal at the bottom of the tank that lifts when you flush and drops back down to stop water flow.
- **Fill valve:** Refills the tank with water after flushing. Has a float that tells it when to shut off.
- **Float:** Rises with the water level. When it reaches the set height, it shuts off the fill valve.
- **Overflow tube:** A vertical tube that prevents the tank from overflowing. If the water level is too high, it drains into the bowl.
- **Flush chain:** Connects the handle to the flapper. When you push the handle, the chain lifts the flapper.

## Step 1: Identify the Problem

Remove the tank lid and flush. Watch what happens:

- **Water keeps running** after the tank fills → flapper issue or fill valve problem.
- **Water runs into the overflow tube** → float is set too high.
- **Hissing sound** from the fill valve → fill valve is worn out.
- **Water in the bowl is colored** (food coloring test) → flapper isn't sealing.

## Step 2: Replace the Flapper (Most Common Fix)

The flapper is the #1 cause of running toilets. Rubber degrades over time, causing it to warp or crack.

1. **Turn off the water** using the valve behind the toilet (turn clockwise).
2. **Flush the tank** to drain the water.
3. **Unhook the old flapper** from the overflow tube and disconnect the chain from the handle arm.
4. **Take the old flapper** to the hardware store to match the size (they cost $3-5).
5. **Install the new flapper** — hook it onto the overflow tube and attach the chain to the handle arm.
6. **Turn the water back on** and let the tank fill. Flush and check for leaks.

> **Pro tip:** If the flapper looks fine but still leaks, try cleaning the flapper seat with a damp cloth — mineral buildup can prevent a good seal.

## Step 3: Adjust the Float

If the water level is too high and spilling into the overflow tube:

- **Ball float (old style):** Bend the float arm down slightly so the float sits lower. This makes the fill valve shut off earlier.
- **Cylinder float (modern):** Squeeze the clip on the float and slide it down the fill valve shaft to the correct height. The water should stop about 1 inch below the top of the overflow tube.

## Step 4: Check the Fill Valve

If the fill valve continues running after the flapper and float are fixed:

1. **Adjust the valve** — most fill valves have a screw on top that controls the water level. Turn it to lower the shutoff point.
2. **If the valve still runs**, replace it. Fill valve replacement kits cost $8-15 and include clear instructions.

## Step 5: Check the Chain

If the flush chain is too tight, it holds the flapper open. If it's too long, it can get caught under the flapper.

- **Ideal length:** The chain should have about 1/2 inch of slack when the flapper is closed.
- **Adjust** by reattaching the chain at a different link or trimming excess with pliers.

## Quick Diagnosis Table

| Symptom | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Constant running | Worn flapper | Replace flapper ($3-5) |
| Water in overflow tube | Float too high | Adjust float |
| Hissing from fill valve | Worn valve | Adjust or replace ($8-15) |
| Color in bowl (test) | Bad flapper seal | Replace flapper |
| Chain caught under flapper | Too much slack | Adjust chain length |

## The Bottom Line

**Replace the flapper first** — it fixes 90% of running toilets and costs under $5. If that doesn't work, check the float height and fill valve. The whole job takes 30 minutes and saves hundreds of gallons of wasted water.
