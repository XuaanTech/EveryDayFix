# How to Stop Condensation on Windows

Waking up to foggy, dripping windows is frustrating — and it's more than just a cosmetic issue. Persistent window condensation can lead to mold, mildew, and damage to your window frames and walls.

The good news is that condensation on windows is usually fixable without expensive repairs. Understanding the cause is the first step.

## Why Windows Get Condensation

Condensation happens when warm, moisture-laden air hits a cold surface. Your windows are often the coldest surface in a room, making them the perfect place for water vapor to condense.

**Common sources of indoor moisture:**
- Cooking and boiling water
- Showers and baths
- Drying clothes indoors
- Breathing (yes, really)
- Houseplants
- Gas appliances

## How to Stop Condensation on Windows

### 1. Improve Ventilation

The simplest fix is to let moist air escape.

- **Open windows** for 10-15 minutes each morning, even in winter.
- **Use extractor fans** when cooking or showering.
- **Open trickle vents** on your windows if they have them.
- **Leave interior doors open** to allow air circulation.

### 2. Use a Dehumidifier

A dehumidifier actively removes moisture from the air.

- Place it in the room with the worst condensation.
- Run it for 2-3 hours each morning.
- Empty the water tank regularly.
- Target indoor humidity of **40-50%**.

> **Budget tip:** Even a small, inexpensive dehumidifier can make a noticeable difference in a bedroom or small room.

### 3. Stop Drying Clothes Indoors

Drying clothes on radiators or indoor racks releases large amounts of moisture into the air.

- Dry clothes outside when possible.
- Use a ventilated indoor dryer in a room with an open window.
- Consider a clothes horse near an open window rather than on a radiator.

### 4. Keep Heating Consistent

Fluctuating temperatures create condensation problems.

- Keep a **low, consistent temperature** rather than heating in bursts.
- A warm room with dry air has less condensation than a hot-and-cold cycle.
- Keep bedroom doors closed during the day and heat them slightly at night.

### 5. Use Moisture Absorbers

For mild condensation issues, moisture-absorbing products can help.

- Place **silica gel packets** or commercial moisture absorbers on windowsills.
- Use **bowl of salt** or **baking soda** as a natural alternative.
- Replace or recharge them regularly.

### 6. Check Your Window Seals

Faulty seals let cold air in and reduce the insulating barrier.

- Check for drafts around window frames.
- Replace worn or damaged weatherstripping.
- Apply window insulation film in winter for an extra barrier.

### 7. Apply Anti-Condensation Coating

Special sprays create a thin layer that reduces surface tension.

- Buy anti-condensation spray from hardware stores.
- Apply to clean, dry glass.
- Reapply every few weeks.

## When Condensation Is a Bigger Problem

If condensation appears **between the panes** of double-glazed windows, the seal has failed and the unit needs replacing. This is different from surface condensation and requires professional repair.

## Quick Fixes vs. Long-Term Solutions

| Approach | Time to Work | Cost | Effectiveness |
|----------|-------------|------|---------------|
| Open windows | Immediate | Free | Good |

| Consistent heating | Days | Varies | Good |
| Moisture absorbers | Days | $5-20 | Moderate |
| Window insulation film | Immediate | $5-15 | Good |

## The Bottom Line

The most effective approach combines **better ventilation** with **moisture control**. Open windows daily, use extractor fans, and consider a dehumidifier if condensation is persistent.

Most condensation problems can be solved without replacing windows — it's about managing the moisture in your home, not upgrading your glazing.
