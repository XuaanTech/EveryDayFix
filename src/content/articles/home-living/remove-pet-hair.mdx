# How to Remove Pet Hair from Furniture and Clothes

Pet hair has a way of turning every surface in your home into a fur coat. Lint rollers work, but they run out fast and barely make a dent on large furniture. There are better, faster methods that use things you already have.

## The Rubber Glove Trick (Best for Furniture)

This is the single most effective method for removing pet hair from upholstery, and it costs nothing if you have dishwashing gloves.

1. **Put on a rubber or latex glove** (the kind you wash dishes with).
2. **Dampen it slightly** — just barely wet, not dripping.
3. **Run your hand** over the furniture surface in long, smooth strokes.
4. **The pet hair will cling to the rubber** and gather into clumps you can pick up.
5. **Rinse the glove** and repeat until all hair is gone.

> **Why it works:** The rubber creates friction and static that pulls hair away from fabric fibers. A dry glove works, but a slightly damp one is even more effective.

## The Packing Tape Method (Best for Clothes)

When you don't have a lint roller, packing tape is just as effective.

1. **Wrap a length of packing tape** around your hand, sticky side out.
2. **Dab the fabric** with your taped hand, pressing firmly.
3. **Peel off the tape** and wrap a fresh section as needed.
4. For large areas, tear off a long strip of tape and press it directly onto the surface.

> **Pro tip:** A lint roller is just tape on a stick — if you're going through lint rollers quickly, switch to packing tape for a fraction of the cost.

## The Rubber Broom (Best for Carpet and Large Areas)

A rubber broom or rubber-bladed squeegee is a game-changer for pet hair on carpet and large upholstered surfaces.

1. **Drag the rubber broom** across the carpet in one direction.
2. **The hair will gather** into clumps that you can pick up by hand or vacuum.
3. **For furniture,** use a squeegee the same way — drag it across cushions and fabric surfaces.

This method removes more hair than a vacuum in a single pass.

## Removing Pet Hair from Clothes in the Wash

Pet hair can survive a regular wash cycle. Here's how to actually remove it:

1. **Before washing:** Run a lint roller or rubber glove over the garment to remove loose hair.
2. **Add 1/2 cup of white vinegar** to the fabric softener compartment — it loosens hair from fabric fibers during the rinse cycle.
3. **Use the tumble dry setting** with a dryer sheet. The static reduction attracts hair to the dryer lint trap.
4. **Clean the lint trap** after the cycle — it will be full of pet hair.

> **Important:** Don't put pet-hair-covered clothes in the dryer without removing hair first. Heat can bake hair into the fabric, making it harder to remove.

## Best Vacuum Attachments for Pet Hair

If you vacuum regularly, the right attachment makes a huge difference:

- **Motorized brush head** — the most effective; the spinning bristles lift hair from deep in carpet fibers.
- **Upholstery tool** — a wide, flat attachment designed for furniture.
- **Crevice tool** — gets hair from corners, between cushions, and along baseboards.

## Prevention: Reduce the Shedding Problem

- **Brush your pet daily** — especially during shedding season. A de-shedding tool (like a Furminator) removes loose hair before it hits your furniture.
- **Use washable furniture covers** — easier to throw in the wash than to clean the entire couch.
- **Keep a lint roller** by the front door for quick touch-ups before leaving the house.
- **Vacuum twice a week minimum** with a pet-hair attachment.

## The Bottom Line

The **damp rubber glove** is the best all-around method — it works on furniture, car seats, and almost any fabric surface. For carpet, invest in a **rubber broom**. For clothes in the laundry, **white vinegar in the rinse cycle** loosens hair that would otherwise survive the wash.
