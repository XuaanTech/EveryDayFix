# How to Clean Grout Between Tiles

Grout lines are magnets for dirt, mildew, and stains. Because grout is porous, it absorbs everything — water, soap scum, food splatter, and grime. Over time, those once-white lines turn dingy gray or even black.

The good news is that you don't need expensive cleaners or harsh chemicals. Two common household items — baking soda and hydrogen peroxide — will restore grout to near-original condition.

## What You'll Need

- **Baking soda** (about 1 cup)
- **Hydrogen peroxide** (3% solution — the standard brown bottle)
- **Water**
- **An old toothbrush or stiff-bristle brush**
- **A spray bottle**
- **Rubber gloves**

## Method 1: Baking Soda + Hydrogen Peroxide (Best Overall)

This is the most effective DIY grout cleaner and works on floor, wall, and countertop tiles.

### Steps:

1. **Sweep or vacuum** the area to remove loose dirt and debris.
2. **Make a paste:** Mix baking soda with a small amount of water until you get a thick, spreadable consistency (like toothpaste).
3. **Apply the paste** directly to the grout lines. Use your fingers (with gloves) or a small spatula.
4. **Let it sit** for 10-15 minutes. For very dirty grout, let it sit overnight.
5. **Spray hydrogen peroxide** directly over the paste. It will fizz — that's the reaction breaking down stains.
6. **Scrub** with an old toothbrush or stiff brush, working the paste into the grout.
7. **Rinse** with clean water and wipe dry with a towel.

> **Why it works:** Baking soda is a mild abrasive that physically lifts dirt, while hydrogen peroxide is a natural bleaching agent that kills mold and lightens stains. Together, they tackle both the cause and the appearance of dirty grout.

## Method 2: Vinegar Spray (For Mild Stains)

For lighter grout staining, vinegar alone may be enough.

1. **Fill a spray bottle** with undiluted white vinegar.
2. **Spray directly** onto the grout lines.
3. **Let it sit** for 5-10 minutes.
4. **Scrub** with a brush and rinse with water.

> **Warning:** Avoid vinegar on natural stone tiles (marble, travertine, limestone). The acid can etch and damage stone surfaces. Use the baking soda method instead.

## Method 3: Steam Cleaning (For Deep Grime)

A steam cleaner with a brush attachment is excellent for grout — the heat loosens dirt without any chemicals.

1. **Fill and heat** your steam cleaner.
2. **Use the brush attachment** to run along grout lines.
3. **Wipe away** the loosened dirt with a cloth as you go.
4. This works especially well in the bathroom and kitchen.

## Sealing Your Grout

Once your grout is clean, protect it with a sealer:

1. **Make sure the grout is completely dry** — wait at least 24 hours after cleaning.
2. **Apply grout sealer** according to the product instructions (usually a brush or spray-on application).
3. **Wipe excess sealer** off the tile surfaces.
4. **Let it cure** for 24-48 hours before getting the area wet.
5. **Reapply every 1-2 years** to maintain protection.

Sealed grout repels water, dirt, and stains — making future cleaning much easier.

## Prevention Tips

- **Seal grout** after installation and reapply every 1-2 years.
- **Wipe up spills** on tiled surfaces immediately.
- **Use a daily shower spray** in the bathroom to prevent soap scum buildup.
- **Ventilate bathrooms** — use the exhaust fan during and after showers to reduce moisture.
- **Mop tiled floors weekly** to prevent dirt from settling into grout lines.

## The Bottom Line

The **baking soda and hydrogen peroxide paste** is the best DIY solution for dirty grout — it's cheap, effective, and non-toxic. Once clean, **seal the grout** to keep it that way and make future cleaning a 2-minute task instead of a 30-minute scrub.
