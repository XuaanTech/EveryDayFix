# Bluetooth Not Connecting? Fix It on Any Device

Bluetooth is one of those technologies that works perfectly — until it doesn't. Your headphones won't pair, your mouse keeps disconnecting, or your phone just won't find your speaker. The good news: 90% of Bluetooth issues are fixed with the same few steps, regardless of device.

## The Universal Fixes (Try These First)

These work on **Windows, Mac, Android, iOS, Linux** — anything with Bluetooth.

### 1. The "Turn It Off and On" Cycle
It sounds basic, but it clears temporary glitches.

1. **Turn Bluetooth OFF** on your phone/computer.
2. **Turn the accessory OFF** (headphones, speaker, mouse).
3. **Wait 15 seconds.**
4. **Turn the accessory ON** and put it in **pairing mode**.
5. **Turn Bluetooth ON** on your phone/computer.
6. **Pair.**

> **Why this works:** Both sides reset their Bluetooth radios and clear any stuck connection states.

### 2. Forget and Re-Pair (Most Effective)
If a device was previously paired, "forgetting" it forces a clean handshake.

**On any device:**
1. Go to **Bluetooth settings**.
2. Find the problematic device in the list.
3. Tap **"Forget," "Remove," or "Unpair."**
4. Put the accessory in **pairing mode**.
5. **Re-pair from scratch.**

> **This fixes:** Corrupted pairing keys, outdated connection parameters, and "ghost" connections where the device thinks it's connected but it's not.

### 3. Restart Both Devices
A full restart clears deeper system-level issues.

- **Phone/Computer:** Full restart (not just sleep/wake).
- **Bluetooth accessory:** Turn off, wait 30 seconds, turn on.
- **Then pair.**

## Device-Specific Fixes

### Windows 10/11

#### Run the Bluetooth Troubleshooter
1. **Settings** → **System** → **Troubleshoot** → **Other troubleshooters**
2. Click **Run** next to **Bluetooth**.
3. Follow any prompts.

#### Restart the Bluetooth Support Service
1. Press **Win + R**, type `services.msc`, press Enter.
2. Find **Bluetooth Support Service**.
3. Right-click → **Restart**.
4. Set **Startup type** to **Automatic**.

#### Reinstall the Bluetooth Driver
1. Right-click **Start** → **Device Manager**.
2. Expand **Bluetooth**.
3. Right-click your adapter (usually **Intel**, **Realtek**, or **Generic Bluetooth**) → **Uninstall device**.
4. Check **Attempt to remove the driver** if prompted.
5. **Restart your computer** — Windows will reinstall it automatically.

#### Check Bluetooth Version Compatibility
- **Bluetooth 5.0+** devices work best with Windows 10 1809+ and Windows 11.
- Older adapters (Bluetooth 4.0) may struggle with newer headphones.

### Mac (macOS)

#### Reset the Bluetooth Module
1. **Option + Shift + Click** the Bluetooth menu bar icon.
2. Select **Reset the Bluetooth module**.
3. Re-pair your devices.

#### Delete Bluetooth Preference Files
1. Open **Finder** → **Go** → **Go to Folder** (`Cmd+Shift+G`).
2. Enter: `~/Library/Preferences/`
3. Delete (or move to desktop):
   - `com.apple.Bluetooth.plist`
   - `com.apple.Bluetooth.plist.lockfile`
3. **Restart your Mac.**

#### Check for Interference
- Macs are sensitive to 2.4GHz interference. If you have WiFi issues too, **switch WiFi to 5GHz** or move the Mac away from the router.

### Android

#### Clear Bluetooth Cache
1. **Settings** → **Apps** → **See all apps**.
2. Tap **three-dot menu** → **Show system apps**.
3. Find **Bluetooth** → **Storage** → **Clear cache** and **Clear storage**.
4. **Restart phone** and re-pair.

#### Check App Permissions
1. **Settings** → **Apps** → **Bluetooth** → **Permissions**.
2. Ensure **Nearby devices** and **Location** are allowed.
3. Android 12+ requires **Location** permission for Bluetooth scanning.

### iOS / iPadOS

#### Reset Network Settings (Nuclear Option)
This clears ALL saved WiFi passwords and Bluetooth pairings.

1. **Settings** → **General** → **Transfer or Reset iPhone** → **Reset**.
2. **Reset Network Settings**.
3. Enter passcode → confirm.
4. **Re-pair everything.**

#### Toggle Airplane Mode
1. **Swipe down** for Control Center.
2. Tap **Airplane Mode** → wait 10 seconds → tap again.
3. This resets all radios including Bluetooth.

## Common Scenarios & Fixes

### "My headphones connect but no sound"
1. **Check output device:** Settings → Sound → Output → select headphones.
2. **Check volume mixer:** Some apps have independent volume.
3. **Disconnect other Bluetooth audio devices** — they may be stealing audio.

### "My mouse/keyboard keeps disconnecting"
1. **Replace batteries** — low battery is the #1 cause.
2. **Move receiver closer** — USB extenders help for desktop PCs.
3. **Unpair other unused Bluetooth devices** — too many connections cause instability.
4. **Disable USB selective suspend** (Windows): Device Manager → USB Root Hub → Power Management → uncheck "Allow computer to turn off this device."

### "My phone won't find the device at all"
1. **Put the accessory in pairing mode** — this is the #1 mistake. Most devices need a specific button hold (power for 5-10 seconds, or power + volume).
2. **Keep them 1 foot apart** during pairing.
3. **Turn off other nearby Bluetooth devices** — they can interfere with discovery.

### "Connected to two devices, won't switch"
Many modern headphones support multipoint. If switching fails:
1. **Pause audio on device A.**
2. **Play audio on device B.**
3. If that fails: **Turn off Bluetooth on device A**, then play on device B.
4. Some headphones need the **app** (Sony, Bose, Sennheiser) to manage multipoint.

## Bluetooth Interference: The Silent Killer

Bluetooth and WiFi **both use 2.4GHz**. They interfere with each other.

| Source of Interference | Fix |
|------------------------|-----|
| WiFi router on 2.4GHz | Switch to 5GHz WiFi |
| Microwave running | Don't use Bluetooth near running microwave |
| Multiple Bluetooth devices | Unpair unused ones |
| USB 3.0 devices | Move Bluetooth dongle away from USB 3.0 ports |
| Wireless cameras/baby monitors | They use 2.4GHz — keep distance |

> **Pro tip:** If you have a desktop PC, use a **USB Bluetooth adapter with an extension cable** to move the antenna away from the case and USB 3.0 ports. It makes a huge difference.

## When to Suspect Hardware Failure

If you've tried everything above and it still fails:

| Symptom | Likely Cause |
|---------|--------------|
| Works on other devices, not this one | Software/driver issue on this device |
| Doesn't work on ANY device | Accessory is broken |
| Works briefly then dies | Battery failing |
| Pairs but immediately disconnects | Firmware issue on accessory |
| Static/crackling audio | Antenna damage or interference |

## The Bottom Line

**Forget and re-pair** fixes 70% of Bluetooth issues. **Restart both devices** fixes another 20%. The remaining 10% need driver updates, interference management, or hardware replacement. Start simple, work up.
