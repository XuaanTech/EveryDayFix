# How to Get Rid of Bad Smells in Shoes

We've all experienced it — you take off your shoes and the room immediately knows about it. Shoe odor is embarrassing, persistent, and can make you self-conscious about removing your shoes anywhere.

The good news is that eliminating shoe smells is straightforward, and preventing them from coming back is even easier.

## Why Shoes Smell

Shoe odor isn't actually caused by the shoes themselves — it's caused by **bacteria** that thrive in the warm, moist environment inside your shoes.

Your feet have approximately **250,000 sweat glands** and can produce up to a pint of sweat per day. When that moisture gets trapped in shoes:

1. Bacteria multiply rapidly
2. They break down sweat and dead skin cells
3. This produces the sulfur compounds we recognize as "stinky feet"

## Method 1: Baking Soda (Easiest & Most Effective)

Baking soda absorbs moisture and neutralizes odor-causing bacteria.

1. **Sprinkle baking soda** generously inside each shoe.
2. **Let it sit overnight** (or for at least 8 hours).
3. **Shake out the powder** in the morning.
4. **Vacuum or tap** to remove any remaining residue.

> **Pro tip:** For extra effectiveness, add a few drops of tea tree oil to the baking soda before sprinkling — it has natural antibacterial properties.

## Method 2: Freezing (Kills Bacteria)

Extreme cold kills the bacteria that cause odor.

1. **Place each shoe** in a sealed plastic bag.
2. **Put them in the freezer** overnight (at least 12 hours).
3. **Remove and let thaw** before wearing.
4. The bacteria are killed, and the odor is eliminated.

> **Why it works:** The cold temperature kills bacteria without damaging the shoes. It's surprisingly effective for persistent smells.

## Method 3: White Vinegar Solution

Vinegar kills bacteria and neutralizes odors.

1. **Mix equal parts** white vinegar and water in a spray bottle.
2. **Spray inside** each shoe thoroughly.
3. **Let them dry** completely (in a well-ventilated area, NOT in direct sunlight).
4. The vinegar smell disappears as it dries.

## Method 4: Dryer Sheets

A quick and easy overnight fix.

1. **Place a dryer sheet** inside each shoe.
2. **Leave overnight** or when shoes are not in use.
3. **Remove in the morning** — the shoes will smell fresh.

## Method 5: Tea Tree Oil

Tea tree oil has powerful natural antibacterial and antifungal properties.

1. **Mix 10 drops** of tea tree oil with 2 tablespoons of water.
2. **Spray inside** each shoe.
3. **Let dry** completely.
4. Repeat as needed.

## Method 6: Coffee Grounds

Coffee grounds absorb odors and moisture.

1. **Place dry coffee grounds** in a small cloth bag or old sock.
2. **Put inside each shoe** overnight.
3. **Remove in the morning.**
4. Reuse the grounds for a few days before replacing.

## Method 7: Cornstarch

Cornstarch absorbs moisture, which reduces bacterial growth.

1. **Sprinkle cornstarch** inside each shoe.
2. **Leave overnight.**
3. **Shake out** in the morning.

## Deep Cleaning: When Smells Persist

If surface treatments don't work, the bacteria may be deep in the shoe material.

### Machine Wash (Fabric/Canvas Shoes Only)
1. **Remove laces and insoles** if possible.
2. Place shoes in a **pillowcase or laundry bag**.
3. Wash on **gentle cycle, cold water**, with mild detergent.
4. **Air dry** — never put shoes in the dryer.

### Replace Insoles
- Old insoles absorb and harbor bacteria.
- **Replace them** with new insoles (available at most shoe stores).
- Or add **antibacterial insoles** specifically designed for odor control.

### Deep Clean Leather/Suede
- Use a **leather cleaner** or **suede brush**.
- Wipe the interior with a cloth dampened with diluted rubbing alcohol.
- Let dry naturally.

## Prevention: Stop Shoe Odor Before It Starts

Prevention is easier than treatment.

- **Wear moisture-wicking socks** — cotton traps moisture; synthetic blends wick it away.
- **Alternate shoes daily** — let each pair dry for 24 hours between wears.
- **Use shoe trees** — cedar shoe trees absorb moisture and odor.
- **Sprinkle baking soda** in shoes regularly (once a week).
- **Keep shoes in a dry area** — avoid damp closets or bags.
- **Wash insoles monthly** — hand wash with soap and water.
- **Choose breathable shoes** — leather and mesh allow airflow; synthetic materials trap moisture.

## The Bottom Line

For immediate results, **sprinkle baking soda inside your shoes overnight** — it works reliably for most people.

For persistent smells, **combine methods**: freeze the shoes to kill bacteria, then use baking soda or tea tree oil for ongoing freshness. And to prevent the problem long-term, **alternate your shoes** and always let them dry completely between wears.
