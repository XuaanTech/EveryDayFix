# How to Sharpen Kitchen Scissors — The Right Way

Dull kitchen scissors don't just make cooking annoying — they're actually dangerous. You end up using more force, which means less control, which means cuts. And let's be honest: struggling to snip through a chicken breast or a tough bag of frozen peas is just embarrassing.

Most people never sharpen their kitchen scissors. They buy new ones when the old ones get dull. But a good pair of kitchen shears can last decades with proper sharpening. Here's how to do it right.

## Why Kitchen Scissors Get Dull

| What Dulls Them | What Happens |
|-----------------|--------------|
| Cutting paper/cardboard | Paper fibers are abrasive; they wear the edge fast |
| Cutting through bones | Micro-chips in the blade edge |
| Dishwasher (don't do this) | Heat and detergent ruin the edge and loosen the pivot |
| Cutting on hard surfaces | Cutting against plates/counters dulls instantly |
| Not cleaning | Food residue corrodes the edge |

## The Golden Rule: Respect the Original Angle

Kitchen scissors have a **much wider angle** than knives. Knives are 15-20° per side. Scissors are **45-60° per side**. If you use a knife sharpener, you'll ruin the scissor edge.

## Method 1: Sharpening Stone (Best Results)

This gives the sharpest, longest-lasting edge.

### What You Need
- **Medium-grit whetstone** (1000-3000 grit) — a combination stone with coarse/medium sides is ideal
- **Water or honing oil** (depending on stone type)
- **Towel** to stabilize the stone

### Steps

1. **Clean the scissors** thoroughly with hot, soapy water. Dry completely.
2. **Take them apart** if they have a screw pivot. (Riveted? Skip this step.)
3. **Soak/prep the stone** per manufacturer instructions.
4. **Hold one blade flat** against the stone at its original bevel angle.
   - The bevel is the angled edge on the **inside** of each blade (the side that faces the other blade when closed).
   - Match the existing angle — don't guess.
5. **Stroke from pivot toward tip** in smooth, even strokes.
   - Push the blade **away from you** (like you're slicing a thin layer off the stone).
   - 5-10 strokes per side is usually enough.
6. **Repeat on the other blade** — same angle, same number of strokes.
7. **Remove the burr:** Lightly run the **flat side** (outside) of each blade flat on the stone once or twice. This knocks off the microscopic burr created by sharpening.
8. **Reassemble** (if taken apart). Apply a drop of food-safe oil to the pivot.
9. **Test on paper** — should cut cleanly without tearing.

> **Pro tip:** Mark the bevel with a black marker before sharpening. As you stroke, the marker will wear off where the stone contacts. Adjust your angle until the marker wears evenly across the bevel.

## Method 2: Ceramic Mug (Surprisingly Good)

The unglazed ceramic ring on the bottom of a coffee mug works as a fine sharpening surface. It's essentially a free ceramic honing rod.

1. **Flip a ceramic mug upside down.**
2. **Find the unglazed ring** on the bottom — it feels rough.
3. **Hold the scissor blade** at the original bevel angle against the ring.
4. **Stroke from pivot to tip** 8-10 times per blade.
5. **Deburr** by lightly running the flat side on the ring once.
6. **Test on paper.**

> **Works best for:** Touch-ups between real sharpenings. Not aggressive enough for very dull scissors.

## Method 3: Diamond Sharpening Rod / Ceramic Rod

If you have a knife sharpening rod, check if it's **ceramic** or **diamond-coated**. Steel rods (honing steels) don't work on scissors — they're too smooth.

1. **Hold the rod vertically** tip-down on a cutting board.
2. **Hold one blade** at the original bevel angle against the rod.
3. **Stroke from pivot to tip** along the rod, 8-10 times.
4. **Repeat on other blade.**
5. **Deburr** on the flat side once.

## Method 4: Aluminum Foil (Quick Fix Only)

This doesn't actually sharpen — it **hones** (realigns the edge). Use when you need a quick fix and have no tools.

1. **Fold a sheet of aluminum foil** 4-6 times thick.
2. **Cut through the foil** with the scissors 10-15 times.
3. **Wipe the blades clean.**
4. **Test on paper.**

> **Reality check:** This cleans the blades and can temporarily improve a slightly dull edge. It will NOT fix genuinely dull scissors. Don't rely on it.

## Method 5: Sandpaper (In a Pinch)

1. **Use 400-600 grit wet/dry sandpaper** on a flat surface.
2. **Hold the blade** at the bevel angle and stroke from pivot to tip.
3. **5-8 strokes per blade.**
4. **Deburr** on the flat side.
5. **Clean thoroughly** — you don't want sandpaper grit in your food.

## Special Situations

### Serrated Kitchen Scissors
Some kitchen shears have one serrated blade (for gripping slippery food).

- **Only sharpen the straight (non-serrated) blade.**
- The serrated blade does the gripping; the straight blade does the cutting.
- Sharpen the straight blade using any method above.

### Poultry Shears / Bone-Cutting Scissors
These have thicker, stronger blades and sometimes a notch for cutting bones.

- **Use a coarse stone first** (400-800 grit) to repair chips from bone cutting.
- **Finish on medium stone** (1000-3000 grit).
- **Maintain the wider angle** — these are often 50-60° per side.

### Scissors That Won't Cut After Sharpening

If they're sharp but still won't cut:
1. **Check the pivot tension** — too loose = blades don't contact properly. Tighten the screw slightly.
2. **Check for bent blades** — sight down the edge. Bent blades need professional repair or replacement.
3. **Clean the pivot area** — built-up gunk prevents full closure.

## Maintenance: Keep Them Sharp Longer

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Hand wash and dry immediately | Put in dishwasher |
| Cut on cutting boards | Cut against plates, pans, counters |
| Use for food only | Cut paper, cardboard, plastic packaging |
| Oil the pivot monthly | Force through bones they're not rated for |
| Store open (not jammed in a drawer) | Store with other metal tools banging against them |

## When to Replace Instead

- Blades are **chipped or cracked** (not just dull)
- The pivot is **riveted and loose** (can't be tightened)
- Blades are **bent** and won't align
- You've sharpened them so many times the blades are thin
- They're **cheap stamped steel** — not worth the effort

> **Good kitchen shears worth sharpening:** Wüsthof, Zwilling, Joyce Chen, OXO Good Grips, Shun. These have quality steel that takes and holds an edge.

## The Bottom Line

**Sharpening stone = best results.** Ceramic mug = good touch-up. Aluminum foil = emergency band-aid. The key is respecting the original wide angle (45-60°) and sharpening the **inside bevel** of each blade. Do it every 6-12 months and your kitchen scissors will outlast your knives.
