# How to Clean White Sneakers

White sneakers look amazing when they're fresh out of the box — and terrible after a few weeks of wear. Dirt, grass stains, scuffs, and general grime make them look worn-out long before they actually are.

The good news: you can restore them to near-new condition with things you already have at home. No special sneaker cleaner required.

## What You'll Need

- **Dish soap** or laundry detergent
- **Baking soda**
- **A soft-bristle brush** (an old toothbrush works perfectly)
- **White paper towels**
- **Warm water**

## Step-by-Step Cleaning

### Step 1: Prep the Sneakers

1. **Remove the laces and insoles.** Set them aside — you'll clean them separately.
2. **Shake off loose dirt** by tapping the soles together.
3. **Dry-brush** the sneakers with your brush to remove caked-on mud or dust.

### Step 2: Clean the Upper

1. **Mix warm water** with a few drops of dish soap.
2. **Dip your brush** in the solution.
3. **Scrub the entire upper** in small circular motions, focusing on stained areas.
4. For mesh and canvas, you can scrub more vigorously. For leather, use gentler pressure.
5. **Wipe with a damp cloth** to remove soap residue.

### Step 3: Clean the Soles

The rubber soles and midsoles collect the most grime.

1. **Make a paste** of baking soda and water (2:1 ratio).
2. **Apply the paste** to the rubber soles with your brush.
3. **Scrub thoroughly** — the mild abrasive action of baking soda lifts dirt and restores whiteness.
4. **Wipe clean** with a damp cloth.

### Step 4: Deal with Yellowing

Yellow stains on white canvas come from glue seepage or UV exposure:

1. **Mix baking soda** with hydrogen peroxide to form a paste.
2. **Apply to the yellowed area** with a brush.
3. **Place the sneakers in direct sunlight** for 30-60 minutes.
4. **Rinse off** the paste. The combination of peroxide, baking soda, and UV light bleaches the yellow away.

### Step 5: Dry Properly

1. **Stuff the sneakers** with white paper towels to absorb moisture and maintain shape.
2. **Air dry** in a well-ventilated area away from direct heat (no radiators, hair dryers, or direct sun for extended periods).
3. **Never put sneakers in the dryer** — heat warps the shape and melts glue.

### Step 6: Clean the Laces

1. **Soak laces** in warm, soapy water for 15-30 minutes.
2. **Scrub them** between your hands.
3. **Rinse and air dry.** Or place in a mesh laundry bag with your next laundry load.

## Material-Specific Tips

| Material | Safe to Scrub? | Special Notes |
|----------|----------------|---------------|
| Canvas | Yes, vigorously | Most durable — can handle baking soda and peroxide |
| Mesh | Yes, gently | Be careful not to tear mesh with aggressive scrubbing |
| Leather | Gentle only | No baking soda on leather — use damp cloth + mild soap |
| Suede | No scrubbing | Use a suede eraser or brush only — water damages suede |
| Rubber soles | Yes, vigorously | Baking soda paste works great |

## Prevention

- **Apply fabric protector spray** before the first wear — it creates a barrier against stains.
- **Wipe down sneakers** after each wear with a damp cloth.
- **Store in a cool, dry place** away from direct sunlight to prevent yellowing.
- **Use shoe trees** or stuff with paper to maintain shape.
- **Rotate your sneakers** — wearing the same pair every day accelerates wear.

## The Bottom Line

For most white sneakers: **dish soap + toothbrush** for the upper, **baking soda paste** for the soles, and **paper towel stuffing** for drying. For yellowed canvas, the **baking soda + hydrogen peroxide + sunlight** method works remarkably well. Always **air dry** — never use heat.
