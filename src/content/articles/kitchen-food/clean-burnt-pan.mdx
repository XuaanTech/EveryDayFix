# How to Clean a Burnt Pan

You turn away from the stove for just a moment, and suddenly there's a blackened, seemingly ruined pan staring back at you. Burnt-on food is one of the most common kitchen mishaps — but it's almost always fixable.

Here are the best methods to restore your pan, whether it's stainless steel, non-stick, cast iron, or aluminum.

> ⚠️ **Burn risk:** Several of these methods involve **boiling water and hot pans**. Always use oven mitts when handling hot cookware, keep the handle turned away from the edge of the stove, and **let the pan cool before scrubbing** — never submerge a hot pan in cold water, as it can warp the metal and send up hot steam. Keep children and pets away from the stove while working.

## Method 1: Baking Soda and Water (Safest for All Pans)

This is the go-to method that works on virtually any type of pan.

1. **Remove loose debris** — tip out any burnt bits.
2. **Add water** to cover the burnt area (about 1 inch deep).
3. **Add 2 tablespoons of baking soda.**
4. **Bring to a boil** on the stove over medium heat.
5. **Simmer for 5-10 minutes**, stirring occasionally.
6. **Remove from heat** and let it cool.
7. **Scrape gently** with a wooden spoon or silicone spatula.
8. **Wash normally** with dish soap and a sponge.

> **Pro tip:** For very burnt pans, let the baking soda solution sit overnight before scrubbing.

## Method 2: Vinegar and Baking Soda (For Stubborn Burns)

The chemical reaction between vinegar and baking soda creates fizzing that lifts burnt residue.

1. **Pour enough white vinegar** to cover the burnt area.
2. **Bring to a simmer** over medium heat for 1-2 minutes.
3. **Remove from heat.**
4. **Add 2 tablespoons of baking soda** — it will fizz vigorously.
5. **Let it sit** for 10-15 minutes.
6. **Scrape with a wooden spoon** and wash normally.

## Method 3: Boiling Water and Dish Soap (For Light Burns)

For lighter burns, this gentle method often works.

1. **Fill the pan** with enough hot water to cover the burnt area.
2. **Add a generous squirt** of dish soap.
3. **Bring to a boil** and let it simmer for a few minutes.
4. **Let it cool** and check if the residue has loosened.
5. **Scrub with a non-abrasive sponge.**

## Method 4: Salt Scrub (For Stainless Steel Only)

Salt is a gentle abrasive that works well on stainless steel.

1. **While the pan is still warm** (not hot), sprinkle a generous layer of coarse salt.
2. **Add a small amount of water** to make a paste.
3. **Scrub with a sponge** or crumpled aluminum foil.
4. **Rinse and wash** normally.

> ⚠️ **Do not use this on non-stick or coated pans** — salt can scratch and damage the surface.

## Method 5: Dryer Sheet Trick (For Light Burns)

Surprisingly effective for light to moderate burns.

1. **Fill the pan** with warm water.
2. **Add a dryer sheet** and push it under the water.
3. **Let it soak** for 1-2 hours (or overnight for tough burns).
4. **Remove the sheet** and scrub gently.

## Method 6: Hydrogen Peroxide (For Stainless Steel)

1. **Cover the burnt area** with hydrogen peroxide (3% solution).
2. **Add baking soda** to form a paste.
3. **Let it sit** for 30 minutes to an hour.
4. **Scrub and rinse.**

> ⚠️ **Safety:** Hydrogen peroxide can irritate skin and eyes and is harmful if swallowed. Wear **gloves**, avoid getting it in your eyes or mouth, work in a well-ventilated area, and **keep children and pets away** while it's in use. Rinse surfaces thoroughly afterward.

## Cleaning by Pan Type

| Pan Type | Best Method | Avoid |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Stainless Steel | Baking soda + vinegar, salt scrub | Nothing really — very durable |
| Non-Stick | Baking soda soak, dryer sheet | Steel wool, abrasive scrubbers |
| Cast Iron | Baking soda + salt scrub | Dish soap (de-seasons), soaking |
| Aluminum | Baking soda + water | Vinegar (can pit the surface) |
| Copper | Lemon juice + salt | Abrasive pads |

## How to Prevent Burning

- **Use medium heat** — high heat doesn't cook faster, it burns faster.
- **Don't leave pans unattended** when cooking at high temperatures.
- **Use enough fat** — oil, butter, or cooking spray creates a barrier.
- **Match pan to burner** — a small pan on a large burner heats unevenly.
- **Deglaze promptly** — if food starts sticking, add a splash of water or broth.
- **Preheat properly** — heat the pan first, then add oil, then add food.

## When to Replace the Pan

Sometimes a pan is genuinely beyond saving:

- **Non-stick coating is peeling or scratched** — replace for health reasons.
- **Warping** — a warped pan won't cook evenly.
- **Deep pitting** in aluminum or stainless steel.
- **Persistent discoloration** that won't clean off and affects cooking.

## The Bottom Line

**Baking soda and water** is one of the safest and most effective methods for cleaning burnt pans. It works on most types of cookware and requires only items you already have.

For the worst burns, combine **vinegar and baking soda** — the fizzing reaction does most of the work for you.
