# How to Store Fruits and Vegetables to Make Them Last

Half your grocery money goes in the trash when produce wilts, goes soft, or molds before you eat it. The fix isn't buying less — it's storing each item correctly.

Different fruits and vegetables have very different storage needs. One wrong placement — a banana next to your lettuce, or tomatoes in the fridge — can cut shelf life in half.

## The Golden Rule: Ethylene Gas

Many fruits release **ethylene gas** as they ripen — a natural plant hormone that triggers ripening (and eventually spoilage) in nearby produce.

**High ethylene producers:** Bananas, apples, avocados, tomatoes, melons, stone fruit.
**Sensitive to ethylene:** Leafy greens, broccoli, carrots, cucumbers, peppers, berries.

**Keep them apart.** One banana on the counter is fine. A bag of apples next to your lettuce is not.

## Storage Guide by Item

### Fruits

| Fruit | Where to Store | How | Lasts |
|-------|---------------|-----|-------|
| Bananas | Counter | Wrap stems in plastic wrap to slow ripening | 5-7 days |
| Apples | Fridge (crisper) | In a plastic bag with holes | 4-6 weeks |
| Berries | Fridge | In original container, unwashed | 3-7 days |
| Grapes | Fridge | In original bag, unwashed | 1-2 weeks |
| Avocados | Counter (unripe) → Fridge (ripe) | Ripen on counter, then refrigerate | 3-5 days ripe |
| Tomatoes | Counter | Stem-side down, away from sunlight | 5-7 days |
| Citrus | Fridge (crisper) | In mesh bag or loose in drawer | 2-4 weeks |

### Vegetables

| Vegetable | Where to Store | How | Lasts |
|-----------|---------------|-----|-------|
| Leafy greens | Fridge | Wash, dry thoroughly, store in paper-towel-lined container | 5-7 days |
| Carrots | Fridge | Remove tops, store in a jar of water (change water every few days) | 2-3 weeks |
| Celery | Fridge | Wrap in aluminum foil (not plastic) | 2-3 weeks |
| Broccoli | Fridge | In an open bag in the crisper | 5-7 days |
| Bell peppers | Fridge | In crisper drawer, unwashed | 1-2 weeks |
| Mushrooms | Fridge | In original container or paper bag (not plastic) | 5-7 days |
| Fresh herbs | Fridge (most) or counter (basil) | Upright in a jar of water | 1-2 weeks |

### Pantry Items (Cool, Dark, Dry)

| Item | Store With | Don't Store With |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Potatoes | In a dark pantry, in a breathable bag | Onions (gas causes sprouting) |
| Onions | In a dark pantry, in a breathable bag | Potatoes |
| Garlic | In a cool, dry place, in a mesh bag | Nothing special |
| Sweet potatoes | Cool, dark, dry spot | Nothing special |

## Pro Tips

- **Paper towel trick:** Line container bottoms with paper towels to absorb excess moisture — this extends the life of greens, berries, and cut vegetables significantly.
- **Don't wash berries until ready to eat.** Moisture is the enemy — pre-washed berries mold faster.
- **Wrap celery and carrots in foil,** not plastic. Foil allows moisture to escape while plastic traps it.
- **Remove banana stems from the bunch.** Bananas release the most ethylene at the stem — wrapping it in plastic slows the process.
- **Check produce every 2-3 days.** One moldy strawberry can spoil an entire container. Remove bad pieces immediately.

## The Bottom Line

Separate ethylene producers from sensitive vegetables, store most produce in the fridge crisper, and keep pantry items in a cool, dark place. A small investment in **airtight containers and paper towels** can double the life of your fresh produce.
