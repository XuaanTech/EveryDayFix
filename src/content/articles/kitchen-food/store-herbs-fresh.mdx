# How to Keep Fresh Herbs from Wilting

You buy a bunch of fresh basil or cilantro, use a few leaves for dinner, and two days later it's a wilted, slimy mess in the back of your fridge. Sound familiar?

The good news is that most fresh herbs can last 1-3 weeks with the right storage — and sometimes months if you freeze them.

## The Fridge Method (Most Herbs)

This works for parsley, cilantro, dill, chives, mint, and most other herbs (except basil).

1. **Trim the stems** at a 45-degree angle — fresh cuts absorb moisture better.
2. **Wrap the bunch loosely** in a slightly damp paper towel. Not dripping — just barely damp.
3. **Place the wrapped herbs** inside a zip-lock bag or airtight container. Don't seal it completely — leave a small opening for air circulation.
4. **Store in the crisper drawer** of your fridge.
5. **Replace the paper towel** every 3-4 days if it gets too wet.

> **Why it works:** The damp paper towel keeps the herbs hydrated without drowning them. Herbs wilt because they lose moisture, but they rot when they're too wet. This method finds the balance.

## The Water Jar Method (Basil)

Basil is the exception to almost every rule. It hates cold temperatures and wilts faster in the fridge.

1. **Trim the stems** at a 45-degree angle.
2. **Place the bunch** in a jar or glass with an inch of water — like a bouquet of flowers.
3. **Loosely cover** with a plastic bag.
4. **Leave on the counter** (not in the fridge) at room temperature.
5. **Change the water** every 2 days.

Basil stored this way lasts about a week and stays vibrant and aromatic.

## Freezing Herbs (Long-Term Storage)

If you have more herbs than you can use fresh, freezing them preserves the flavor for months.

### Olive Oil Method (Best for Cooking)

1. **Finely chop** the herbs.
2. **Divide into portions** — about 1 tablespoon each.
3. **Place in an ice cube tray.**
4. **Cover each compartment** with olive oil.
5. **Freeze until solid** (about 2 hours).
6. **Transfer cubes** to a labeled zip-lock bag.
7. **Use directly in cooking** — toss a cube into a pan when sautéing.

### Water Method (Versatile)

Same as above, but use water instead of oil. Better for adding herbs to soups, stews, or sauces.

### Dry-Freeze Method (Best for Garnishing)

1. **Spread herbs in a single layer** on a baking sheet.
2. **Freeze for 1 hour** until solid.
3. **Transfer to a zip-lock bag** and crumble slightly.
4. **Sprinkle directly** onto finished dishes.

## Reviving Wilted Herbs

If your herbs have already started to wilt, you can often bring them back:

1. **Fill a bowl** with ice water.
2. **Submerge the herbs** completely.
3. **Let them soak** for 15-30 minutes.
4. **Remove and pat dry** with a paper towel.
5. **Store properly** using one of the methods above.

This works best for leafy herbs like parsley, cilantro, and mint. It's less effective for herbs that have turned slimy — those should be composted.

## Storage Guide by Herb

| Herb | Best Method | Lasts |
|------|-------------|-------|
| Basil | Water jar, room temp | 5-7 days |
| Parsley | Fridge, damp paper towel | 1-2 weeks |
| Cilantro | Fridge, damp paper towel | 1-2 weeks |
| Mint | Fridge, damp paper towel | 1-2 weeks |
| Dill | Fridge, damp paper towel | 1 week |
| Chives | Fridge, damp paper towel | 1-2 weeks |
| Rosemary | Fridge, damp paper towel | 2-3 weeks |
| Thyme | Fridge, damp paper towel | 2-3 weeks |

## The Bottom Line

For most herbs, the **damp paper towel in the fridge** method extends freshness to 1-2 weeks. For basil, use the **water jar method** on the counter. For long-term storage, **freeze herbs in olive oil** — they'll be ready to drop straight into your next meal.
